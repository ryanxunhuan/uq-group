Bayesian Optimal Experimental Design

Which data should we acquire?

A useful experiment begins with a clear learning objective. Bayesian optimal experimental design expresses that objective through the expected value of potential observations, using a model of the experiment and the information already available.

Our work develops formulations and algorithms for nonlinear models, uncertain predictions, and sequences of experiments. Goal-oriented methods focus data collection on a specified quantity of interest; sequential methods adapt to observed results while accounting for future opportunities.

Evaluating a design can require many simulations and repeated inference. We develop computational approximations and learned policies that address this cost, and study stopping decisions and risk-sensitive objectives alongside the choice of measurements.

  • Goal-oriented design Target the parameters, predictions, or causal quantities needed for a particular question.
  • Sequential design and stopping Coordinate experiments over time and balance further learning against its cost.
  • Computational design Estimate and optimize design objectives using variational, simulation-based, and multifidelity methods.

Scientific Machine Learning and Inverse Problems

What can we learn about scientific systems from data?

Scientific learning involves more than fitting an input–output relationship. The task may be to identify an equation, infer an unobserved property, or predict a system under conditions that are difficult to measure directly.

We investigate Bayesian inference, system identification, physics-informed learning, and probabilistic predictive models. These approaches use different kinds of scientific knowledge, from governing equations to symmetries and statistical relationships.

A model’s meaning depends on its assumptions and the data that support it. Our work examines how measurement noise, limited observations, and imperfect model structure affect what can be inferred, and how these uncertainties carry through to quantities of scientific interest.

  • Inverse problems and model discovery Infer parameters and governing structure from observed system behavior.
  • Physics-informed and structured learning Use equations, symmetries, and domain knowledge in learned models.
  • Probabilistic prediction Represent uncertainty in learned predictions and assess it against observations.
  • Model inadequacy Represent the effects of imperfect scientific models when calibrating parameters and making predictions.

Computational Uncertainty Quantification

How can we quantify uncertainty when models are expensive?

A prediction depends on uncertain inputs, imperfect models, and the numerical methods used to evaluate them. Quantifying their effects often requires repeated calculations across many possible scenarios.

Our work develops multifidelity methods that combine simulations with different costs and capabilities, and surrogate models that approximate expensive responses. Adaptive sampling directs new calculations toward information that improves the approximation or estimator.

Computational efficiency is tied to the quantity being estimated and the available budget. We study how to allocate simulations, account for uncertainty in the information used to make that allocation, and connect these methods to inference and experimental design.

  • Multifidelity estimation Combine models of different costs to improve statistical estimation under a computational budget.
  • Surrogate modeling and active learning Approximate expensive responses and select new simulations that improve the approximation.
  • Uncertainty propagation and sensitivity Trace how uncertain inputs and model assumptions affect predictions.
