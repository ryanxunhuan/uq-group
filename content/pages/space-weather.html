Forecasting Space Weather with Predictive Uncertainty

GeoDGP learns from solar wind and magnetometer observations to forecast ground magnetic disturbances. Its probabilistic predictions describe uncertainty alongside the forecast, connecting scientific machine learning with a consequential geophysical problem.

Published · Space Weather · 2025

The Scientific Question

Geomagnetic disturbances can induce currents in power grids and other infrastructure. Forecasts of the ground magnetic field help characterize these events, but observations are unevenly distributed and forecast uncertainty varies across regions.

Our Approach

GeoDGP uses deep Gaussian processes and represents location relative to the Sun through solar magnetic coordinates. It learns relationships between upstream observations and ground magnetic responses, producing predictions with uncertainty estimates. The study evaluates multiple storms and stations against physics-based and data-driven forecasting models.

What This Work Shows

The results demonstrate probabilistic regional forecasting from geophysical observations. A public website displays forecast products, while subsequent work investigates symmetry-based modeling for the Southern Hemisphere.

Scope of the Study

The original 2025 study identifies limitations in auroral-zone uncertainty, local conductivity effects, and Southern Hemisphere validation. Predicting infrastructure impacts requires additional models beyond the magnetic-field forecasts.

Related work: mirror-symmetry encoding for Southern Hemisphere forecasts extends this line of research.
