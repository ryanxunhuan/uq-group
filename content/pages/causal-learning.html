Designing Interventions for a Causal Question

A causal experiment should address the scientific question behind it. We learn adaptive intervention policies that target a specified causal quantity and account for what later experiments could reveal.

Published · Transactions on Machine Learning Research · 2026

The Scientific Question

Experiments can reveal cause and effect, but collecting interventional data is costly. A study may seek a particular causal effect while other features of the underlying network remain uncertain.

Our Approach

GO-CBED assigns value to interventions through their expected information about a chosen causal quantity. It learns a policy for a sequence of experiments, adapting each intervention to earlier observations. A transformer represents the experimental history, while normalizing flows approximate posterior distributions for a tractable training objective.

What This Work Shows

Tests on synthetic causal models and simulated gene-regulatory networks show how focusing on a specific question and planning ahead can improve learning under limited experimental budgets.

Scope of the Study

Results depend on the assumed causal models, prior knowledge, and learned approximations. The gene-network studies use simulated experiments; performance in laboratory campaigns remains to be assessed.
