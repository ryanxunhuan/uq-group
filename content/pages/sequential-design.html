Choosing Experiments and Deciding When to Stop

An experiment campaign can adapt both its next measurement and its stopping point. We study policies that weigh future learning against cost, making termination part of Bayesian experimental design.

Preprint · 2025, revised June 2026

The Scientific Question

Experimental results can change which measurement is most useful next. They can also change whether collecting more data is worthwhile. Planning an entire campaign therefore involves both the sequence of experiments and the decision to end it.

Our Approach

We formulate these choices as a finite-horizon sequential decision problem. A value-based stopping rule compares the reward for ending now with the expected value of continuing. Policy-gradient learning and value approximation provide a computational approach, while a training curriculum gradually introduces stopping to reduce premature termination during learning.

What This Work Shows

Numerical studies include inference benchmarks and simulated contaminant-source detection. They show how jointly learning design and stopping policies can balance information gain against experimental cost, particularly when early decisions shape later measurement opportunities.

Scope of the Study

The policies are learned numerical approximations, evaluated in simulated experiments. Their performance depends on the model, learning objective, experimental costs, and training.

The Paper

C. Cheng and X. Huan (2025), Optimal stopping for sequential Bayesian experimental design. Available at arXiv:2509.21734.
