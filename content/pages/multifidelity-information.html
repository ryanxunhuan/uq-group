Estimating Information Gain with Models of Different Costs

Ranking experiments can require thousands of costly simulations. We combine detailed and inexpensive models to reduce the sampling noise in information-gain estimates while preserving the high-fidelity numerical target.

Published · SIAM/ASA Journal on Uncertainty Quantification · 2025

The Scientific Question

Bayesian design ranks experiments by what they are expected to teach us. Computing that information gain can itself require many simulations, making detailed physical models costly to use.

Our Approach

We reformulate the information-gain calculation so that models of different fidelities can share random inputs. Approximate control variates then combine their outputs, using model correlations and computational costs to allocate simulations under a fixed budget.

What This Work Shows

A nonlinear benchmark and a turbulent-flow study demonstrate reduced sampling variance relative to single-fidelity estimates. The flow example evaluates measurements for learning uncertain turbulence-model parameters.

Scope of the Study

The estimator preserves the mean of the high-fidelity numerical utility; approximating that utility can still introduce bias relative to exact information gain. Efficiency depends on model correlation, relative cost, and allocation estimates.

The Paper

T. E. Coons and X. Huan (2025), A multifidelity estimator of the expected information gain for Bayesian optimal experimental design, SIAM/ASA Journal on Uncertainty Quantification 13(4), 1990–2021.
