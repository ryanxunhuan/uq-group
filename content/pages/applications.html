Applications and Collaborations

We collaborate across science and engineering to connect observations, scientific models, and decisions. These examples show how shared questions about learning and uncertainty arise in different systems.

Scientific questions determine which uncertainties are important, what can be measured, and how predictions will be used. We welcome collaborations in new application areas and work with domain experts.

Environmental Hazards and Space Weather

Which observations improve a forecast?

Projects address flood prediction, monitoring, and resilience through FLOODWARRIOR ↗ (opens in a new tab) (MURI), alongside probabilistic space-weather forecasting.

  • Placing flood sensors around a prediction goal
  • Forecasting space weather with predictive uncertainty
  • Matching urban flood sensor placement to monitoring objectives using Bayesian optimal experimental design
  • GeoDGP: One-hour ahead global probabilistic geomagnetic perturbation forecasting using deep Gaussian process

Aerospace and Fluid Dynamics

How can limited simulations support useful predictions?

Alongside work on propeller noise and turbulent flows, we participate in C-PRIME ↗ (opens in a new tab) (PSAAP IV), a center advancing predictive modeling for rotating detonation combustors.

  • Learning uncertain fields with fewer expensive simulations
  • Data-driven propeller noise prediction approach with transfer and active learning
  • Multi-fidelity uncertainty quantification of particle deposition in turbulent pipe flow

Energy Systems

What can available measurements reveal about system condition?

Research on batteries and wind power examines how learned models and physical information can inform health assessment and forecasting.

  • Inferring battery health from partial charging data
  • Real-time physics-aware battery health monitoring from partial charging profiles via physics-informed neural networks
  • Inverted transformers for effective post-calibration in multisite wind power forecasting

Materials and Manufacturing

Which mechanisms and model terms explain the observations?

Studies of microstructure evolution and industrial coating processes connect system identification and interpretable learning with models of material behavior and manufacturing processes.

  • Learning governing equations from noisy observations
  • Variational system identification of the partial differential equations governing microstructure evolution in materials: Inference over sparse and spatially unrelated data
  • Enhancing dynamical system modeling through interpretable machine learning augmentations: A case study in cathodic electrophoretic deposition

Health and Biological Systems

How can measurements and mechanistic models inform biological understanding?

Collaborations include using wearable sensors to assess the intensity of balance exercises and detect loss-of-balance events during everyday walking, and using inverse reinforcement learning within mechanistic models of cancer cell heterogeneity.

  • Automatic multi-IMU-based deep learning evaluation of intensity during static standing balance training exercises
  • Automated loss-of-balance event identification in older adults at risk of falls during real-world walking using wearable inertial measurement units
  • Integrating inverse reinforcement learning into data-driven mechanistic computational models: A novel paradigm to decode cancer cell heterogeneity

Human Behavior and Decision Making

How do uncertainty and limited data affect human-centered models?

Research examines how people interpret uncertainty in human–AI decisions, how data scarcity affects models of human behavior, and how well LLM moderation guardrails align outputs with expert user expectations.

  • Understanding uncertainty: How lay decision-makers perceive and interpret uncertainty in human-AI decision making
  • A Bayesian approach for quantifying data scarcity when modeling human behavior via inverse reinforcement learning
  • “Do your guardrails even guard?” Method for evaluating effectiveness of moderation guardrails in aligning LLM outputs with expert user expectations
