Research Stories

Bayesian Optimal Experimental Design

Choosing Experiments and Deciding When to Stop

An experiment campaign can adapt both its next measurement and its stopping point. We study policies that weigh future learning against cost, making termination part of Bayesian experimental design.

Placing Flood Sensors Around a Prediction Goal

A flood sensor’s value depends on what its observations should help predict. We compare locations using Bayesian experimental design, linking the monitoring objective to the information expected from a measurement.

Designing Interventions for a Causal Question

A causal experiment should address the scientific question behind it. We learn adaptive intervention policies that target a specified causal quantity and account for what later experiments could reveal.

Scientific Machine Learning and Inverse Problems

Learning Governing Equations from Noisy Observations

Observed fields contain clues about the equations governing a system. We combine weak-form system identification with Bayesian inference to select candidate terms and quantify uncertainty in model coefficients and derived physical quantities.

Forecasting Space Weather with Predictive Uncertainty

GeoDGP learns from solar wind and magnetometer observations to forecast ground magnetic disturbances. Its probabilistic predictions describe uncertainty alongside the forecast, connecting scientific machine learning with a consequential geophysical problem.

Inferring Battery Health from Partial Charging Data

A physics-informed model uses partial charging data to infer internal aging-related parameters. Those parameters help estimate battery health and connect measured voltage to the cell’s electrochemical behavior.

Computational Uncertainty Quantification

Learning Uncertain Fields with Fewer Expensive Simulations

Predicting an entire flow field can be costly. We combine inexpensive and detailed simulations in a bifidelity surrogate, then use active learning to select additional simulations where they are expected to improve prediction.

Estimating Information Gain with Models of Different Costs

Ranking experiments can require thousands of costly simulations. We combine detailed and inexpensive models to reduce the sampling noise in information-gain estimates while preserving the high-fidelity numerical target.

Deciding When Enough Pilot Simulations Have Been Run

Multifidelity estimation needs preliminary simulations to learn which models work well together. We quantify uncertainty in those relationships and use it to decide when to end pilot sampling and begin final estimation.
