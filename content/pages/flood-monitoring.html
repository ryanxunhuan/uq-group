Placing Flood Sensors Around a Prediction Goal

A flood sensor’s value depends on what its observations should help predict. We compare locations using Bayesian experimental design, linking the monitoring objective to the information expected from a measurement.

Preprint · August 2026

The Scientific Question

A flood monitoring system might aim to learn model parameters, forecast water depth at a particular location, or estimate conditions across a region. These goals can favor different measurements, even within the same flood event.

Our Approach

Using simulations of the August 2014 metropolitan Detroit flood, we compare sensor locations by expected information gain about parameters or selected predictions. A neural-network surrogate makes repeated flood-model evaluations practical.

What This Work Shows

In this study, local depth targets favor nearby measurements, while regional targets can favor nonlocal locations. These findings suggest defining the prediction objective before ranking sensor locations, then weighing information value alongside practical deployment considerations.

Scope of the Study

This is a model-based, single-sensor comparison for one event and urban domain. Operational recommendations require field checks and further assessment of model, measurement, and numerical uncertainty.

The Paper

C. Cheng, V. N. Tran, J. Dong, S. Whitaker, S. Bergt, J. Ziker, V. Y. Ivanov and X. Huan (2026), Matching urban flood sensor placement to monitoring objectives using Bayesian optimal experimental design. Available at arXiv:2608.21182.
