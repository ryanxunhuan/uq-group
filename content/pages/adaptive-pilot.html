Deciding When Enough Pilot Simulations Have Been Run

Multifidelity estimation needs preliminary simulations to learn which models work well together. We quantify uncertainty in those relationships and use it to decide when to end pilot sampling and begin final estimation.

Preprint · August 2025

The Scientific Question

Allocating simulations across models requires knowing how their outputs vary together. Too few pilot runs leave these relationships uncertain; too many consume resources needed for the final estimate.

Our Approach

We treat the model-output covariance matrix as uncertain and update a Bayesian distribution as pilot results arrive. An expected-loss criterion balances the benefit of better covariance estimates against the cost of collecting them, guiding an adaptive decision to stop pilot sampling.

What This Work Shows

Tests on a benchmark and a Darcy-flow model demonstrate useful variance reduction with limited pilot budgets. The method also describes uncertainty in predicted estimator performance, rather than relying on one covariance estimate.

Scope of the Study

Results depend on prior choices, statistical assumptions, and approximations used to anticipate future pilot data. The additional computation is most attractive when model evaluations are expensive.

The Paper

T. E. Coons, A. Jivani and X. Huan (2025), Bayesian covariance uncertainty for adaptive pilot-sampling termination in multi-fidelity uncertainty quantification. Available at arXiv:2508.18490.
