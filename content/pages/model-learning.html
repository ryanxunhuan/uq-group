Learning Governing Equations from Noisy Observations

Observed fields contain clues about the equations governing a system. We combine weak-form system identification with Bayesian inference to select candidate terms and quantify uncertainty in model coefficients and derived physical quantities.

Preprint · June 2026

The Scientific Question

Learning a governing equation requires identifying its structure and coefficients from imperfect observations. Measurement noise affects both these choices and the physical quantities predicted by the inferred model.

Our Approach

Bayesian Variational System Identification evaluates how well observed fields satisfy candidate equations in weak form. It models how observation noise propagates into equation residuals, uses those residuals to select model terms, and approximates coefficient posteriors under the selected model. This avoids repeatedly solving the forward equations during inference.

What This Work Shows

Studies of Fokker–Planck and Cahn–Hilliard systems recover governing terms from noisy simulated data and propagate coefficient uncertainty to potentials and free-energy derivatives. The examples connect equation discovery with uncertainty in scientifically meaningful quantities.

Scope of the Study

The method requires sufficiently rich spatial observations and a specified candidate library. Reported posteriors condition on the selected model; noise assumptions and likelihood approximations affect their interpretation.

The Paper

C. Huang, S. Srivastava, K. Garikipati and X. Huan (2026), Bayesian variational system identification with weak-form residual likelihoods. Available at arXiv:2606.14942.
