Learning Uncertain Fields with Fewer Expensive Simulations

Predicting an entire flow field can be costly. We combine inexpensive and detailed simulations in a bifidelity surrogate, then use active learning to select additional simulations where they are expected to improve prediction.

Preprint · November 2025

The Scientific Question

Uncertain inputs can change a simulated field throughout space. Repeated detailed simulations are expensive, while a low-cost model may miss important features. A useful surrogate must connect input uncertainty to the field while using detailed calculations selectively.

Our Approach

The method combines Karhunen–Loève expansions, which represent spatial variation compactly, with polynomial chaos expansions linking the representation to uncertain inputs. Low-fidelity simulations establish response trends and high-fidelity simulations correct systematic differences. An active-learning procedure uses estimated generalization error to select new high-fidelity evaluations.

What This Work Shows

Tests on analytical, convection–diffusion, and turbulent-jet examples show improved accuracy and sample efficiency relative to the single-fidelity and random-sampling comparisons studied.

Scope of the Study

The evidence is numerical and problem-specific. Performance depends on how informative the low-fidelity model is and how well the surrogate and its error estimate represent the response.

The Paper

A. Jivani, C. Safta, B. Y. Zhou and X. Huan (2025), Bifidelity Karhunen–Loève expansion surrogate with active learning for random fields. Available at arXiv:2511.03756.
