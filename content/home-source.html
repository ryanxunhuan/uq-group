Uncertainty Quantification & Scientific Machine Learning Group

We develop mathematical and computational methods for scientific learning and decision-making under uncertainty.

Led by Xun Huan at the University of Michigan, our group connects Bayesian reasoning, scientific models, and data. Our work asks:

  • Which experiments and observations are most informative?
  • What can we learn about a scientific system from data?
  • How can we quantify uncertainty when models are expensive?

Our Research

Bayesian Optimal Experimental Design

Which data should we acquire?

Experiments are valuable when they resolve uncertainties relevant to a scientific goal. We develop Bayesian methods for choosing measurements, coordinating experiments over time, and deciding when further data collection justifies its cost.

Explore this theme →

Scientific Machine Learning and Inverse Problems

What can we learn about scientific systems from data?

Observations can reveal a system’s parameters, governing mechanisms, and future behavior. We develop learning and inference methods that connect these observations with scientific structure, including physical models and probabilistic descriptions of uncertainty.

Explore this theme →

Computational Uncertainty Quantification

How can we quantify uncertainty when models are expensive?

Uncertainty analysis can require far more computation than a single simulation. We develop estimators, surrogate models, and adaptive sampling methods that make effective use of computational resources while preserving the information needed for a scientific question.

Research Stories

Experimental design

Choosing Experiments and Deciding When to Stop

An experiment campaign can adapt both its next measurement and its stopping point. We study policies that weigh future learning against cost, making termination part of Bayesian experimental design.

Read the story →

Scientific learning and inference

Learning Governing Equations from Noisy Observations

Observed fields contain clues about the equations governing a system. We combine weak-form system identification with Bayesian inference to select candidate terms and quantify uncertainty in model coefficients and derived physical quantities.

Read the story →

Computational UQ

Learning Uncertain Fields with Fewer Expensive Simulations

Predicting an entire flow field can be costly. We combine inexpensive and detailed simulations in a bifidelity surrogate, then use active learning to select additional simulations where they are expected to improve prediction.
